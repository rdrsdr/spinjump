SpinJump

A physics simulation of a rope spiral — not a guide to doing it

SpinJump simulates what happens when a body tied to a rope wound around a tall chimney leaves the top edge: it falls, swings around the structure as the rope unwinds, spirals down and finally hits and slides on the ground. Everything here happens to a virtual crash-test dummy or sandbag inside a physics engine.

Before you continue

  • This is only a simulation. The results come from a simplified computer model and have not been checked against real experiments.
  • It is not a recommendation or instructions. Nothing on this site tells you how to practise this, and no parameter set shown here is "safe".
  • The real activity can kill or seriously injure. Falls from height, collisions with the structure and high-speed impacts with the ground are all part of what the model shows.
  • Do not attempt this — not on your own, not with friends, not "just a small version".

What is being simulated

The rope starts wound in a tight helix near the top of the chimney (50 m tall, 5 m wide by default), unwinding from the bottom coil. The dummy is launched from the edge. As it swings around the chimney the rope peels off the coils, so the free part gets longer and the dummy descends in a widening spiral. The rope lying on the wall is simulated with wall friction: when the pull is steep, the coils slide down the chimney and that friction is what brakes the descent. The model computes rope tension, slack, wrapping, coil sliding, air drag and wind, contact with the wall and the ground, and reports impacts, how far the dummy slid, and where it came to rest. You can change heights, rope, launch, wind and ground and watch the result live — each visitor runs their own simulation on the server.

What this is

  • A physics toy and engineering curiosity built on the MuJoCo physics engine.
  • A way to explore why the spiral happens and how parameters change it.
  • Qualitative and exploratory: trends, not precise numbers.

What this is not

  • Not a sport tutorial, training material, or endorsement.
  • Not an engineering, rigging or safety assessment.
  • Not a prediction of what would happen to a person.

Real-world dangers

  • Falling from height. A free fall from 50 m reaches about 31 m/s (≈ 113 km/h) at the ground. Any failure of the rope, its anchor, a knot or a harness leads to exactly that.
  • High-speed ground impact even when "everything works". In the model's default run the dummy still reaches the ground at about 10 m/s (≈ 36 km/h), mostly sideways, then tumbles and slides for metres.
  • Hitting the structure. Many launch settings swing the body into the chimney wall.
  • Rope, anchor and attachment loads. Swinging and jerks multiply forces far beyond body weight; friction can cut or melt rope.
  • Entanglement and strangulation by a rope moving at speed, and suspension trauma if left hanging.
  • Wind and gusts change the path unpredictably.
  • The structure itself. Chimneys and towers can be unstable or crumbling, may vent hot or toxic gases, and are usually private, restricted property. Climbing them is dangerous and often illegal.
  • Rescue is hard. Injuries at height or on an isolated structure delay help.

Why the numbers are not safety numbers

  • The dummy is a single rigid body; real bodies, tissue and injuries are not modelled.
  • The rope model is simplified: the rope on the wall is a chain of points sliding on the surface with simple Coulomb friction, rope coils only push on each other vertically, and the free part is a straight elastic segment. Friction between the rope and the wall strongly controls how fast the dummy descends, and a real rope could behave very differently.
  • How far the dummy slides after landing is chaotic in this model: tiny changes in the launch change it by tens of percent.
  • Impact peaks (shown in g) depend on numerical contact settings. The OK / WARNING / DAMAGE labels compare them with an illustrative threshold — they are not injury criteria.
  • The model has not been validated against real tests of this activity.

Never attempt this

Do not try this or any variation of it. Do not climb chimneys, towers or other structures to test it. Work at height is done only by trained, certified professionals with engineered anchors, inspected equipment, permits and a rescue plan — and this simulator is no substitute for any of that. If you see someone about to attempt a dangerous climb or jump, contact local emergency services. If you or someone you know is in crisis, reach out to local emergency services or a crisis line.

How the simulator works here

  • Physics and 3D rendering run on the server; your browser only shows the frames and numbers.
  • Each visitor gets their own simulation. The server runs a limited number at once; if it is full, wait a moment and try again.
  • Reports are temporary and anyone with the link can open them. Do not type personal information into the simulation name or parameters.
  • Playback runs in real time by default; if the server is busy the screen shows the actual rate.
